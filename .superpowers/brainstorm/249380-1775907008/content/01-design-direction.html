ALL RETRO

A. Full Retro-Tech Immersion

Every screen becomes part of the Walkman universe. Mood tab gets VU meters and analog dials. Insights shows data on green-phosphor CRT screens. Settings becomes a hardware config panel with toggle switches. Tab bar is a cassette deck control strip.

Pros

  • Totally immersive experience
  • Unique and memorable
  • Strong brand identity

Cons

  • Highest complexity
  • Potential usability trade-offs
  • Every new feature must fit the aesthetic
📼
🎵
😊
📊
⚙️
HYBRID

B. Retro Core + Modern Periphery (Recommended)

Music-related screens (Now Playing, Playlists, Queue) are full retro Walkman. Mood and Insights get retro-inspired accents (monospaced type, brushed metal cards, subtle CRT scanlines) but stay functionally modern. Settings remains clean and usable. A custom cassette-styled tab bar ties it all together.

Pros

  • Best of both worlds
  • Music experience stays immersive
  • Data/utility screens stay usable
  • Consistent design language throughout

Cons

  • Requires careful balancing
  • Must define clear rules for "how retro"
📼
🎵
😊
📊
⚙️
FOCUSED

C. Polish What Exists

Keep the Walkman on Now Playing and cassette grids on Playlists, but don't extend the retro theme further. Focus instead on refining what's already built — smoother animations, better transitions between tabs, micro-interactions, and visual polish. Other tabs get a modern glass-and-gradient treatment.

Pros

  • Least work
  • Lower risk
  • Keeps utility screens clean

Cons

  • Can feel disjointed
  • Two different design languages
  • Less impactful overhaul
